Traditional data centres and networks were not built for inference, let alone superintelligent models too capable for the public internet.

The modern internet was designed to move information, not to produce intelligence. It was built around human-scale workloads: web pages, files, email, search queries and streaming video.

Inference is a computational production workload. A large language model generates tokens through repeated transformations of enormous state, and every token depends on the tokens that came before it. Inference is punished by every extra hop, because every hop increases the physical cost of maintaining state.

Agentic systems make this even sharper. One user action can become hundreds or thousands of inference events, and latency compounds through the whole chain.

We are rethinking the physical data centre and its networking from the ground up.
