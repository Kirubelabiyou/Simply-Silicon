Simply Silicon turns latent utility infrastructure and own-use power into bare metal inference compute, faster than conventional developers.

The internet was built to move information between people. Inference is a different job. Every answer a model gives is produced on the spot, token after token, from a huge amount of model state. It rewards memory, bandwidth and proximity. It is punished by distance at every hop.

Agentic AI makes this sharper. One user action can turn into hundreds of model calls, tool calls and checks. Latency stops being a small delay and starts compounding through the whole chain.

So we build where the demand is. Our sites sit inside cities, on infrastructure that already holds large power connections. They reach nearby buildings over private fiber that never touches the public internet.

Our long term goal is to connect these sites into a second internet for superintelligent models, so they don't break the internet humans need.

From the Simply Silicon mission

Five decisions, made together.

A site is only as strong as its weakest layer. We design power, compute, fiber, security and engineering as one system, so every layer makes the others stronger.

Power

We start where big power already exists: inside the city.

Most new data centres wait years for a grid connection. We skip that wait by building inside utility sites that already have one.

These sites hold large power connections, more than one grid feed and on-site generation. If one source fails, another takes over. Using power that is already in place is faster, and it wastes less than building a new plant far away.

n+1 redundancy
One more of every critical part than the site needs, so any single part can fail.
Own-use power
Power a site already holds for itself, turned into compute instead of sitting idle.

Compute

Dedicated GPU servers that answer in real time.

GPUs do many calculations at the same time, which is exactly what AI models need. We run them bare metal. The customer gets the physical machine, not a slice shared with strangers through a virtual layer.

Our sites are built for inference: the moment a trained model is used to answer a question, write something or make a decision. High-density GPU compute, offered as bare metal or through an API.

Inference
Using a trained model to produce an answer. It is what people feel every time they ask an AI something.
Bare metal
A physical server for one customer, with no shared layer in between. More control, steadier speed.

Fiber

Private cables from our sites to the buildings that use them.

Each site connects to the buildings around it over private fiber that runs under the streets. Data does not cross the shared public internet or common carrier networks to reach the servers.

Short, direct paths mean low latency. Answers arrive fast, and they arrive at a steady, predictable speed. Latency reliability SLAs support applications where a delayed answer is a service failure.

Latency
The delay between asking and getting an answer. Lower is better, most of all for live AI.
Common carrier network
The shared telecom networks everyone else uses.

Security

Physically separate from the public internet.

An air-gapped network has no connection to the public internet, so there is no route in for anyone outside. This is physical separation, not only software rules. A setting can be changed by mistake. A cable that does not exist cannot be hacked.

It suits regulated enterprise, high-security workloads, and AI models too capable for the open internet that need high assurance containment.

Air gap
A network with no physical link to outside networks. Data only moves where cables actually run.
Regulated enterprise
Companies bound by strict rules on data, like banks, utilities and energy producers.

Engineering

More useful AI from every megawatt.

We design the building, the cooling and the network together, so power goes into AI work, not overhead. GPUs turn power into heat, and we capture that heat instead of wasting it.

In Calgary, Foundation sits inside a district heating plant. Its liquid-cooled racks feed a heat exchanger tied to the plant. We build in phases, so capacity grows with demand.

District heating
One central plant heating many buildings through underground pipes.
Phased build
Adding capacity in steps inside the same site, only as it is needed.
