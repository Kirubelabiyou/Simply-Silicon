Two different physical problems.

The internet was built to distribute information between humans. Inference infrastructure must produce intelligence between machines.

The internet moves what we already know.

Web pages, files, email, search and video are stored somewhere, requested and sent. The network rewards reach, redundancy and scale, and it tolerates distance as the price of all three.

Inference produces what we don't know yet.

Tokens are generated one after another from huge model state, and every token depends on the ones before it. It rewards memory locality, bandwidth and thermal control, and it is punished by distance at every hop.

Every hop is a tax.

Agentic AI turns one user action into hundreds of model calls, tool calls and checks. Latency doesn't just slow the answer. It compounds through the whole chain.

A 50 ms penalty, repeated across a chain of machine-speed calls, becomes a real economic cost.

The industry is already rebuilding around locality.

None of today's inference techniques exist to move state faster across distance. Every one of them exists to avoid moving it.

FlashAttention
Fewer reads and writes to memory.
Kernel fusion
Avoids round trips between memory and compute.
Quantization
Shrinks how many bits have to move.
Prefix caching
Reuses state instead of recomputing it.
Cache-aware routing
Sends the request to where the state already lives.
Disaggregation
Splits prefill and decode onto the hardware each needs.

“The winning systems are not the biggest clusters. The systems that place memory, compute, routing, and power closest to the demand pattern will win.”
